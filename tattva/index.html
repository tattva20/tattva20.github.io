Tattva Music — a prototype of a producer's Mexico

Founded in the summer of 2012, Tattva Music was never only a record label. It was an attempt to imagine a Mexico that builds technology instead of only importing it — a cultural brand reaching for its own app, its own store, its own digital identity. It failed as a business. It succeeded as a vision.

The founding — summer 2012

After wanting it for a long time, in the summer of 2012 I finally dared to found Tattva Music. The logo, the color palette and the typography were created by my friend Leonor Hernández, who offered her technical and professional support to the project at every step. The Sanskrit name — tattva, "the essence of things" — was a promise, not decoration.

But the part I want to tell here is not the roster or the discography — that story lives on the Music page. This is the story of what Tattva Music was trying to become, and why it still matters to me even though it never paid off in money.

More than a label

It is one thing to "have a label" in the traditional sense: release tracks, upload them to Beatport, move through the scene, sign artists, do brand promotion. It is a very different thing to say: let's build our own iOS app for the label. That was no longer only music. It was culture + software + brand + distribution + digital identity.

In a country where technology is so often consumed as something imported — the American platform, the Asian hardware, the foreign SaaS, the global brand — the Tattva team was doing something unusual: a Mexican cultural brand trying to own its own technological layer.

"Why does a Mexican label have to limit itself to using everyone else's platforms? Why can't we have our own app, brand, catalogue, experience, narrative and technology? Why should Mexico only assemble, resell, consume or imitate?"

A producer's Mexico, not only a consumer's

And yes — Mexico has talent and already has relevant startups, especially in fintech and digital services. It is not a total desert. But structurally it still sits far from the countries that turn knowledge into global technology brands. Mexico ranks 58th of 139 economies in the Global Innovation Index 2025, and WIPO measures that position as innovation capacity, not merely "there are smart people here." R&D spending tells the same story: the World Bank puts the world near 2.6% of GDP in 2023 and the OECD near 2.93%, while Mexico sits well below those levels.

So even though Tattva Music did not win as a business, the gesture was visionary. Maybe it failed economically, but conceptually it was pointing in a very powerful direction.

Building a small ecosystem of my own

Tattva Music was one of my attempts to not stay stuck in "I'm a DJ." I pushed it toward a record label, then toward our own online store, then toward our own app, then toward software, then toward the idea of a full multimedia company. Trying, as always, to waste nothing — no experience left behind.

  • Label — a home for Mexican and international artists.
  • Store — our own online storefront, not only someone else's marketplace.
  • App — a native iOS app for the label. This was the hinge.
  • Software — the app is what made me start thinking about returning to software.
  • Multimedia — audio software, audiovisual, video games, a broader cultural company.

It was that Tattva Music app that made me begin to imagine going back to software and turning the label into something larger. Tattva Music was one of my first attempts to build a small ecosystem of my own — a prototype of an alternative country, a Mexico where the artist does not only create content but also builds technology, brand, distribution and intellectual property.

A failure as a business, a success as a vision

Tattva Music maybe did not make me rich — in fact I ended up on the credit bureau's blacklist because of this venture. But sometimes the projects that don't pay in money pay in identity, direction and future capabilities. It helped turn me into the person I could later become: an engineer, a systems architect, a technical interviewer, a builder of AI-assisted pipelines.

And there is a quiet irony in it: precisely because it forged those capabilities, we climbed out of the financial hole this venture dug for us faster than expected — and then we truly prospered.

"Tattva Music failed as a business, but not as a vision. It was a prototype of an alternative country."

The door left open

Maybe one day I'll pick up the music story where it was left pending. But if I do, it will be a technological and cultural project too — not a step backward, but the same idea with better tools.

I still keep the ability to publish music on Beatport, Apple Music and other stores. So if someone has material they'd like to place on those platforms, reach out — I might just be up for releasing it.
